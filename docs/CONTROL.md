# The control lab: the model

What the control lab simulates and how, for someone who would otherwise build the model themselves. [GUIDE.md](GUIDE.md) says how to use it; [LIMITS.md](LIMITS.md) section 4 says what it leaves out, in order of importance; [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md) has what it is checked against; [CONTROL_RESEARCH.md](CONTROL_RESEARCH.md) lists the published work the schemes and explorations come from.

The lab closes the loop in time: a permanent-magnet motor and its shaft, the three-leg stage, and a sampled controller wired from blocks. Every wire is recorded. The currents and commutations of the run then go to the same loss and thermal model the rest of the tool uses, so a control scheme is read by what it costs the switches: loss, ripple, junction temperature.

It runs on your own machine only (`motionsim serve`, `docker compose up`, or `motionsim control` from a terminal). The five steps of the page do not use it: they keep the settled result, as before.

## The motor and the shaft

A permanent-magnet synchronous machine in the rotor's frame, with peak (amplitude-invariant) quantities, as everywhere in the tool. State: the d- and q-axis currents, the electrical angle and the mechanical speed.

```
Ld · did/dt = vd − Rs·id + ωe·Lq·iq
Lq · diq/dt = vq − Rs·iq − ωe·(Ld·id + ψ)
Te          = 1.5 · p · (ψ·iq + (Ld − Lq)·id·iq)
J · dωm/dt  = Te − TL(ωm, θm, t)
dθe/dt      = ωe = p · ωm
```

The stage's voltage stands still in the stator's frame while the rotor turns under it. Over a stretch with the switches in one state the voltage seen from the rotor is

```
vd =  vα·cos θe + vβ·sin θe
vq = −vα·sin θe + vβ·cos θe ,   θe(t) = θe0 + ωe·t
```

The currents are taken through each such stretch by the classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta step, with the voltage turned to the angle of each of its stages, in as many steps as keep the step short against the winding's time constant and the turn of the rotor. The speed is held through the stretch and then moved by the torque at its two ends, and the angle by the speed at its two ends: the shaft is far slower than a switching period.

The load at the shaft is the design's own load brought through its gear (its static torque at each speed and angle, and its inertia), or a constant, a viscous and a quadratic torque given directly. The opposing torque passes through nought over a small band about standstill, so a shaft at rest is not thrown back and forth.

The simulated motor can be made to differ from the one the controller was set up for: a factor of 0.2 to 5 on its resistance, each inductance and its magnet flux (`mismatch`). The controller keeps the design's figures.

## The stage: two fidelities

| Fidelity | What the motor sees | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| `switched` (the default) | A triangular carrier. A period runs from the carrier's peak (every leg low) to its valley (every leg high) and back, so a leg of duty cycle d rises (1 − d)/2 of a period after the peak and falls as long before the next. Between two edges the switches stand in one state and the motor is taken through that stretch as it is. | The answer: ripple, the current at each commutation, each switch's own conduction time |
| `averaged` | The mean voltage of the three duty cycles, held through the period. | Fast runs of a loop's dynamics. It has no ripple and no leg states |

A scheme with no modulator (`actuation.kind: states`) drives the legs' states themselves, held through each sampling period, in either fidelity.

Once a drive trips (the `trip` test), the scheme no longer drives the stage. Shorted, the legs stand in the state with every low switch on. With every gate off, each leg conducts through the diode its current's sign picks: into the motor through the low one, at −v_drop, out of it through the high one, at v_dc + v_drop. A leg with no current floats while its terminal stands between the rails. The legs are taken through each period in short steps, sixteen at least and no more than 0.02 rad of electrical angle each. In each step the voltage of a floating leg is the one that keeps its current at nought, found from the plant itself, so it holds for saturating iron as for constant inductances. A conducting leg whose current reaches nought within a step is stopped there, its diode then blocking. A frozen encoder is seen, where it is seen at all, by the check a drive can make from what it has: the voltage it applied through each period and the currents it sampled. It integrates the stator's flux, ψs = ∫(v − Rs·i), from the flux the encoder and the currents put at the first instant, and takes the active flux, ψa = ψs − Lq·i, which lies along the rotor's d axis whatever the saliency. It flags the encoder once ψa stands more than 30° from the encoder's angle three periods running, and judges only while the back-EMF, |ψa| times its own speed of turning, is above 5% of the phase voltage the link allows; below that speed the stage's own voltage errors are as large, and the verdict says the encoder went unseen. The integral leaks with a corner a twentieth of the slowest speed judged, which keeps it from drifting and leads the angle by atan(corner/ωe). The controller reads the frozen angle; the trace keeps the rotor's true angle and speed. A leg whose high switch has failed open stands, while it is told high, where its current puts it: high through the upper diode while the current comes out of the motor, low through the lower one while it goes in, open at nought, taken in sixty-fourths of a period. The drive's check reads each phase current's mean over the last electrical turn against the current's amplitude over it and flags the leg past a fifth: a healthy phase means nought, one that has lost its half-wave about −1/π. It judges only once it has a whole turn of samples and a twentieth of the current limit. `inverter.safe` carries the safe state on the bus (0 switching, 1 off, 2 shorted), and `inverter.i_link` the current the stage returned to the link through the period before.

Two things are off until asked for (`inverter`): with `dead_time`, a leg's voltage through the dead time is the one the current's direction gives, so the edge the current does not commutate by itself comes that much late; with `device_drop`, a device's drop is a fixed voltage against the current in each phase and a resistance in series with the winding. With both off the simulated stage is ideal, and the loss model still charges the dead time in use.

## Sampling, delay and update

- `timing.update: single` samples once a carrier period, at its peak, where the ripple passes through its mean. `double` samples at the peak and the valley.
- `multi` samples and updates `timing.per_carrier` times a carrier period (4, 6 or 8), as a timer does whose compare values are reloaded mid-slope. In the switched stage each slice sets each leg against the carrier at its own duty cycle: where the new one puts the leg on the other side of the carrier, the leg changes at the slice's start, and can cross again within it, so the legs switch a little more often than the carrier. In the averaged stage each slice gets the mean voltage the carrier lets through in it, the share of the slice the carrier stands under the duty cycle, which is not the duty cycle; a whole carrier period at one duty cycle comes to it exactly. The two stages agree at four updates and part at eight (the run says so); the switched one is the reference.
- `every` updates once every `timing.carriers` carrier periods (2 to 8): a controller slower than its stage, as at a high switching frequency. Each carrier period is switched alike at the duty cycles of the update.
- Neither is offered to a scheme of `states`, one that sets its own period, or one that reads shunts (refused by name). A sweep varies the rate by `scheme.rate`, updates a carrier period (0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8), with the current regulator's and speed regulator's gains and the shaping of torque and speed tuned again by the same rules at each; a discrete-time regulator, designed per sample, keeps its own. The linear analysis takes the sampling period and delay as they are: at several updates a period it takes the modulator's mean gain, where only the slices the carrier crosses in answer to a change.
- `timing.delay: true` applies what is computed at one instant from the next: one sampling period of computation. With the half period the voltage is held for, the delay around the current loop is 1.5 sampling periods (0.5 with the delay off), and the inverse Park transform advances the angle by as much.
- A scheme of `states` is sampled at `timing.f_control`. The presets set it to 5 times the design's switching frequency.
- A scheme may set its own carrier period, instant by instant, through a wire named in `actuation.period`: it is held within a quarter and four times the nominal period.
- A drive that starts running begins on its settled duty cycles, held through the first period, before any command of the run can reach the stage.
- **Shunts in the stage.** For a scheme with a `shunt_sampling` block, and only then, the kernel also puts on the bus what shunts could read of the period before: each leg's low-switch time about the instant (`inverter.window_a`, `_b`, `_c`), and for one shunt in the DC link the current at the end of each of the period's two active vectors, which legs were high and how long it stood (`inverter.i_dc_1`, `vector_1`, `span_1`, and the same for the second). Switched, these come from the edges as they fell, dead time included; averaged, from the duty cycles, the current taken where each vector ends along the period's straight line. Every other scheme reads nought there and steps as before. Where the block asks for the shift, the kernel moves the timer's edges as a drive's PWM timer does (`shunt_sampling` above).

At each sampling instant the kernel puts what the plant is doing on its own wires (`plant.*`, `cmd.*`, `time.t`), steps the blocks in the order the scheme lists them, records every wire, and takes the plant through the period. A block that reads a wire driven further down the list reads what was on it one instant ago.

A phase current past the trip level, or one that is no longer a number, ends the run as a refusal that says the loop is unstable. A run of more than 500 000 sampling instants or 5 s is refused before it starts.

## The blocks

A scheme is a wiring of blocks in plain data. Every block has named inputs and outputs, parameters with a unit and bounds, a definition in words and its mathematics; `POST /api/control/presets` gives all of it for every kind under `blocks`. There are 25 kinds. A parameter a preset sets carries the rule its value came from (`sources`), and that is the last column here.

Each observer has two forms. `exact` uses the voltage really applied through the last period and solves the observer's own equation through the period for an input that turns at the estimated speed. `euler` is as firmware often has it: the newest voltage command, one forward-Euler step, the continuous filter's phase. The difference is small at a high carrier ratio and grows to tens of degrees at a ratio of 6.

The supervisor's estimate is the controller's own, as firmware would hold it: a conduction line v(i) = v0 + r·i, a switching-energy line E(i) = e0 + e1·i, and the junction's thermal network as first-order terms, each read once from the loss engine's answer for the device. It is not the loss engine's model, which works the run's real losses out afterwards. The difference between the two is the estimate's error, and `loss_error` makes it larger on purpose.

A block is written against one interface (`motionsim.control.Block`, registered with `register`), so a kind written elsewhere drops into a scheme by its name.

The scheme a design starts on is picked in three steps, and the answer says which applied (`chosen`, `why`). The rule is `presets.PRACTICE` and `presets.chosen`, with its sources by author and title beside it:

1. The application, as such products are built: `servo_joint` for a robot joint, `foc_servo` for a positioning axis (both on an encoder: they hold at standstill), `foc_torque` for a vehicle and a power tool (a torque is asked; the tool on Hall sensors, as it starts loaded), and `sensorless_emf` for a washing machine, a compressor, a vacuum cleaner, drone propulsion, a ceiling fan, a pump or fan and a conveyor (no rotor sensor, for cost, weight or because the shell is sealed, and an I-f start). With no application: `foc_torque` for a motor whose Lq is at least 1.1 times its Ld, `foc_servo` otherwise. A made-up part with no application given takes the one it is tried on. Where the design gives no inertia, or no current limit for an open-loop start, the answer says what was taken in its place and that the loops were tuned on it.
2. The start: a scheme with no encoder is kept only where the current limit's torque, 1.5·p·ψ·I, is more than the load's mean torque on a shaft leaving rest. Otherwise `foc_servo`, on a sensor; a compressor keeps its scheme, and the answer says a larger current limit is needed.
3. The carrier ratio, by the rule the verdict judges a run by (`carrier_need`, `carrier_meets`, `carrier_scheme`): under 30 switching periods in a turn of the output at the design speed a scheme must be sampled twice a period or have a current regulator designed at the sampling rate, under 12 the latter. A speed scheme that does not becomes `foc_high_speed` or `discrete_high_speed`, a torque scheme `discrete_torque`; the schemes with no encoder meet both already.

**Compared side by side.** The lab's Choose for me runs the recommended scheme, the one in the lab and up to three alternatives through `/api/control/run`, in parallel, each as tuned for the same design, test and fidelity. The alternatives take the same test and answer the same command (a speed, or a torque), and are picked to differ most: another way of knowing the angle in the same class, the other class (a rotor sensor or none), another current regulator, then another wiring. Each row is read from the run's own figures (`quality.loops`, `quality.loss.total`, `quality.tj.high` or `peak`, `quality.ripple`, `step`, `timing.f_control`). The best of a row is marked unless it is within 3 % of another (2° of phase margin, 1 K of junction); then they tie. The choice is made among the schemes with no failing finding that deliver at least 95 % of the largest mean torque, and, where the recommended scheme has no rotor sensor, among those with none: the one best on the most of loss, junction, THD, phase margin, settling and torque ripple, the recommended one on a tie. Bandwidth and the count of blocks are shown, and marked, but do not decide alone.

A scheme can be edited and sent back whole. A kind of block that does not exist, a parameter out of its bounds, an input left open or a wire nothing drives is refused by its place in the scheme, such as `scheme.blocks.current.kp_q`.

## The tests

| Kind | What is run | What it is judged on |
|---|---|---|
| `hold` | The drive settled at the design speed and torque; 4 turns of the output analysed by default | Loss, ripple, distortion, junction temperature |
| `torque_step` | The torque asked for steps at a held speed | Rise time (10 to 90%), overshoot, settling time and steady error of the torque |
| `speed_step` | The speed asked for steps, against the load | The same, of the speed |
| `start` | From rest to a speed against the load, at the current limit, the torque coming up hard, slewed or along an S-curve | The current held to the limit, the time to speed |
| `load_step` | The load's torque steps while the speed is held | The largest deviation and the time to recover |
| `stall` | The shaft held still, or turned very slowly, at a high torque, with the rotor at a chosen angle (at −90° phase a carries the whole current); the junctions followed from a cold start | The hottest junction's peak |
| `trip` | The drive running settled at a speed and a torque trips, and the stage goes to its safe state: `off` (every gate off), `short` (the three low switches on) or `hybrid` (the default: shorted above the speed at which the magnet's line voltage, √3·ωe·ψ, passes the DC link and two diodes' drops, off below it, leaving the short only under 95% of that speed). The speed is held through it (`shaft: held`), as the inertia of what the motor turns holds it through the winding's transient; the run lasts five of the winding's time constants after the trip. A load that carries a weight is tripped where that matters most instead: held still with its arm level, on a free shaft (`shaft: free`), shorted, with a holding brake (`brake_torque`, `brake_delay`; where none is given, twice the weight's torque, biting 30 ms after the trip) that stops the shaft and then holds it while what drives it is within its torque. With `cause: encoder` the trip is no longer ordered: the encoder freezes at `t_trip`, the controller reading the angle where it stopped and no speed, and the drive trips the period its own check sees it (below). With `cause: open_switch` leg a's high switch fails open at `t_trip`, and the drive trips the instant its check of the phase currents sees it; the stage is run switched | The most current a phase carried and where it settled (against the closed form of the shorted winding, 0 = Rs·id − ωe·Lq·iq, 0 = Rs·iq + ωe·(Ld·id + ψ)), the braking torque, the current and energy returned to the DC link and, on a supply that takes nothing back, the voltage the link charges toward, √3·ωe·ψ − 2·v_drop, against the device's rating; on a free shaft, how far the load fell, how fast and how far it had fallen when the brake bit; the junction |
| `motion` | A window of the design's own motion (the load and its moves, or a mission profile): the stretch of its peak torque, or of its fastest change of torque, 0.2 s by default. The shaft is free, the load asks what the motion's torque leaves once the shaft's own acceleration is taken out, T(t) − J·α(t), and the scheme is asked the motion's speed, or its torque where it takes only a torque. Between the motion's instants speed and torque are taken in straight lines; the junctions start at the sink's temperature | How far the speed or the torque is from the motion's, at worst and rms, against the most the window asks; the current; the junction |

A scheme that reads no speed command cannot be given a speed step, and one that reads no torque command a torque step: the test is refused with the scheme to take. A step starts from the running drive at its own starting point; `settled` in the answer stays the design point. A window of the motion needs a motion: a design without one is refused by the test.

## The pulsed rating and the observer's floor

**The pulsed rating.** The rest of the tool holds a device's pulsed rating against the settled current with 15% allowed for the current loop passing its reference on a step in torque, and says that the 15% is a fixed allowance. A run of the lab replaces it with the loop's own: after a torque step, the peak of the current's size, √(id² + iq²), read on its mean over a switching period, over the mean it settles to in the last tenth of the run. The rating is held to the settled current times one and that share, with the switching ripple on top, which is the run's own peak; the check names the run's torque step. Any other test makes no step of the current against a reference, so its peak is held with the 15% on top, and the check says it is the allowance. The answer's `pulsed` carries the current held, the rating, the share and whether it was measured.

**The observer's floor.** A scheme with no encoder is given the lowest speed its observer holds the rotor at (`control/floor.py`, the answer's `floor`). It is the larger of the observer's own floor (the back-EMF of 5 Hz electrical the phase-locked loop locks onto; for the flux observer its crossover) and the speed at which the back-EMF is four times what the observer reads it through wrong, the margin the settled result's closed form takes. Three parts are counted: the stage's dead time, t_dead·f_sw·V_dc, and its device's drop, less what dead-time compensation puts back; the current samples' noise and converter step (q/√12), through the resistance and, for the two observers that differentiate the current, the inductance times their filter's bandwidth; and a resistance the controller has wrong, times the design current. The figure is given for the run as simulated (an ideal stage counts no dead time) and for a real stage, with its dead time and drop uncompensated. Beside it: the design speed, the hand-over and hand-back speeds, and, where the request carries the design's motion, the slowest the motion turns, whether it reverses, and the share of its time under the floor. The run's own check that the observer lost the rotor says the floor of this run. A wrong inductance turns the angle by about atan(ΔL·I/ψ) at any speed: an offset, not a floor, and not counted.

## What goes to the loss and thermal model

No loss formula is written in the lab. The loss engine takes a power stage as six roles, one for each switch position: through a period, the current it carries, the share of each switching period it carries it forwards and backwards, whether it commutates, and the current at its turn-on and turn-off. An imported trajectory is turned into those six roles; a simulated run is turned into the same six from what the kernel gathered while it ran:

- the rms of the leg's current over the time the position carried it, so conduction comes from the simulated i(t), ripple included;
- the share of the time it carried it forwards and backwards;
- its commutations, counted, and set against the carrier frequency the engine multiplies by, so a scheme with no carrier is charged for the commutations it really made;
- the mean of the current at its hard turn-ons and at its hard turn-offs.

These are gathered in bins of whole sampling periods. From there on it is the engine: losses and junction temperatures settled together, the reverse path and the dead time split as the engine splits them, and the junctions followed from a cold start as an imported trajectory's are. A scheme that sets its own switching frequency gathers bins of unequal time; they are brought back to equal stretches first, every integral kept.

A run is read by five channels, the ones a control scheme can move: the current the switches carry, how often they commutate, the current they commutate, how far the current is from a sine, and what the junction does. With them come the motor's copper and iron loss over the analysed stretch, each switch's own loss, and for a hold the loss engine's settled answer at the design point to set beside it.

## The linear analysis, and where it parts from the run

`POST /api/control/loops` gives each loop as transfer functions at the sampling rate, in z, with nothing approximated: the regulator's own difference equation, one sampling period of delay where there is one, and the winding seen through a voltage held for a period, sampled.

```
current loop, one axis:   L(z) = C(z) · z⁻ᵈ · (1 − a) / (R · (z − a)),   a = exp(−R·Ts/L)
speed loop:               L(z) = C(z) · T_iq(z) · Ts / (J · (z − 1))
```

with T_iq the closed q-axis current loop. For each loop it gives the open and closed polynomials, Bode data, the gain and phase margins, the −3 dB bandwidth, poles and zeros, and the step it predicts.

It is a second lens. The switched run in time is the answer. The analysis assumes each axis alone (the cross-coupling fed forward exactly, or the rotor still), no voltage limit and no current limit, the angle known exactly, and a shaft with inertia alone, or, where the request gives one, the two-mass shaft that twists (sampled with its torque held over each period, its play left out). A carrier's ripple, a sensor's steps, an observer's error and a controller with no transfer function are outside it: `fcs_mpc`, `dtc` and `deadbeat_dq` schemes give no loops, and the answer says why.

Gains whose closed loop has a pole outside the unit circle are not refused. The run goes ahead, since the voltage limit holds such a loop in an oscillation short of running away; `stability.linear_stable` is false, `stability.said` names the pole, and the checks carry a caution.

For the made-up 100 V MOSFET's starting design (48 V, 20 kHz, 3000 rpm, 4.74 N·m), `motionsim control loops` gives:

```
foc_servo: Cascaded speed and current control (servo)
  the tuning rule: 1000 Hz current-loop bandwidth, a twentieth of the 20,000 Hz sampling rate
  current loop, d axis: bandwidth 2280 Hz, phase margin 62.7 °, gain margin 9.98 dB, largest pole |z| = 0.9825 (stable)
  current loop, q axis: bandwidth 2280 Hz, phase margin 62.7 °, gain margin 9.98 dB, largest pole |z| = 0.9825 (stable)
  speed loop: bandwidth 127 Hz, phase margin 75.1 °, gain margin 23.1 dB, largest pole |z| = 0.9944 (stable)
```

The rule's 1000 Hz is the open loop's crossover it aims for; the closed loop's −3 dB point lies above it.

## Limits

- **Permanent-magnet motors only.** Any other kind is refused.
- **Saturation only where asked for.** The simulated motor's inductances are constants unless its iron is set to saturate (`plant.iron`); the controller's own model is always linear.
- **No iron loss in the simulated motor, no cogging, no back-EMF harmonics, no zero-sequence path.** The torque ripple shown is the current's alone. Iron loss is counted afterwards, from the motor's one figure, taken to follow the square of the stator flux.
- **What the averaged fidelity misses.** Ripple, and so the ripple's share of conduction loss and of the current at each commutation; the legs' states; everything a carrier does to a sample. Use it for a loop's dynamics, and the switched run for any figure of loss.
- **Losses are read in bins.** A bin holds whole sampling periods, so a loss that is not a straight line in the current is read at the bin's mean currents, and a run of more than 8192 sampling periods has several to a bin. A turn-on and a turn-off are charged in pairs; where a bin has one without the other, half a pair is charged.
- **The controller's figures of the stage are two lines.** No temperature dependence, no diode chip of its own, no coupling between chips. That is deliberate for the thermal supervisor, and it also sets how well the loss-weighted predictive cost and the least-loss reference can do.
- **A start with no encoder.** The observers read the back-EMF or the flux it integrates to, and none can start a motor from rest. The start is open loop (align, I-f ramp, hand-over) and the simulated rotor starts aligned; a motor whose axes differ in inductance can instead be started and held at standstill by injection (`sensorless_hfi`).
- **The discrete-time regulator and deadbeat control trust the motor's figures.** Exact for one inductance on both axes; with saliency each axis takes its own and the cancellation is close, not exact. The mismatch explorations show what a wrong figure costs.
- **Left out:** six-step and overmodulation (the voltage is held to the modulation's linear range), optimised pulse patterns, a position loop, induction and reluctance machines, a DC link that moves (the DC voltage is constant), and a braking chopper.
- **Injection on the motor's own inductances and the usual saturation map.** The angle at standstill is read from the incremental inductances the plant has: the controller's two where the iron is linear, the usual map of the motor's kind where it saturates (`plant.iron`). No inverter nonlinearity in the injected signal beyond the dead time the switched stage has, no eddy currents at the injection's frequency, no magnet temperature. Under a load that pulls the shaft (gravity), the rotor is free while the angle is found and its side told, as no holding brake is simulated: "Choose for me" does not pick injection, and it is offered beside the other schemes. The compensation of the axes' coupling is one rate at half the current limit; a drive's table by current is not modelled. A scheme that injects runs on saturating iron unless linear iron is asked for by name, as the side is told from the saturation; on linear iron the side cannot be told and the drive does not start. Under a load that asks more than the current held while injecting, the speed asked is not reached until the observer takes over. The C export refuses the block by name.
- **Shunts read ideally within their window.** A shunt's reading is the true current at the instant, or at its vector's end, quantised and with noise where given: no amplifier ringing, no offset drift with temperature and no common-mode step at the edge, which is what the window stands for. The C export covers the reading and its reconstruction; the timer's shift belongs to the PWM timer and is not in it.
- **Not compared with a measured drive.** The lab is held to closed forms, to the tool's own settled engine and to linear theory. No waveform, loss or temperature from it has been set beside a measurement on hardware.
